        About Mapsplain

        Mapsplain turns a real route into directions the way a local uncle gives them: landmarks, vague-but-honest distances, and “ask anyone after that”. It is a remix of conventional turn-by-turn navigation as popularised by Google Maps, built for the STEAL//REMAKE remix hackathon.

        What we changed

        • Metre counts become conversational distances (“keep going for a bit”). Exact numbers stay in the conventional column.
        • Street names give way to real, mapped landmarks near each turn: temples, petrol pumps, bakeries, trees.
        • Four personas (Local Uncle, Auto Driver, Gen Z Bestie, Auntie) rewrite the same route in their own voice.
        • Side-by-side comparison, read-aloud, landmark match labels, a joke-only “Uncle got confused” button, tree mode and shareable cards.

        How the landmark match label works

        Each landmark gets an internal 0–100 match score from: distance to the turn, whether it has a name, how recognisable its category is, how close it is to the route line, and whether it was already used. It measures how well a map feature fits the turn. It is not a probability that the real place still exists, is open, or is visible from the road.

        Data & services

        • Map tiles, places and landmarks: © OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the Open Database License (ODbL).
        • Map library: Leaflet (BSD-2-Clause).
        • Routing: OSRM via the public demo server (driving) and FOSSGIS routing.openstreetmap.de (walking, and driving fallback).
        • Search: Nominatim (OpenStreetMap Foundation server, used within its usage policy).
        • Landmarks: Overpass API.
        • Share cards: html2canvas (MIT).
        • Voice: your browser’s built-in Web Speech API. Available voices depend on your device.

        Mapsplain is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by Google. No Google logos, assets or data are used. Public services are free community resources with no uptime guarantee: please use them gently.
